# Market Intel Strategies

> **Strategies are hypotheses. Performance determines which ones survive.**

---

# Philosophy

Market Intel does not believe in a single "best" trading strategy.

Markets change.

Strategies that work in one environment may fail in another.

Every strategy should be treated as a testable hypothesis.

The platform exists to discover, measure, refine, and compare strategies over time.

---

# What Is a Strategy?

A strategy is a collection of rules that identifies a specific type of trading opportunity.

A strategy does not contain its own market data collection, scoring engine, or AI reasoning.

Instead, it consumes those shared systems and applies additional logic.

---

# Strategy Lifecycle

Every strategy follows the same process:

1. Define hypothesis.
2. Specify entry conditions.
3. Specify exit conditions.
4. Backtest.
5. Paper trade.
6. Evaluate.
7. Improve or retire.

No strategy should advance based solely on intuition.

---

# Strategy Components

Each strategy should define:

- Name
- Description
- Market focus
- Timeframe
- Entry rules
- Exit rules
- Stop-loss methodology
- Profit targets
- Position sizing guidance
- Required indicators
- Required scores
- Risk considerations
- Performance metrics

# Initial Strategy Library

## Relative Volume Breakout

Hypothesis

Stocks experiencing unusually high trading volume combined with technical breakouts may continue trending in the short term.

Potential Signals

- Relative volume spike
- Breakout above resistance
- Strong momentum score
- Positive catalyst
- Low dilution risk

---

## News Catalyst Momentum

Hypothesis

Meaningful company news can create sustained momentum beyond the initial reaction.

Potential Signals

- Positive catalyst score
- High confidence
- Strong liquidity
- Increasing momentum
- Moderate volatility

---

## SEC Filing Opportunity

Hypothesis

Some SEC filings create opportunities before the broader market fully prices them in.

Examples

- Positive restructuring
- Debt reduction
- Insider purchases
- Favorable financing

This strategy should distinguish between beneficial and harmful filings.

---

## Insider Confidence

Hypothesis

Meaningful insider buying may indicate management confidence.

Potential Signals

- Recent insider purchases
- Strong financial health
- Low promotion risk
- Positive technical structure

---

## Recovery Bounce

Hypothesis

Oversold companies with improving fundamentals may experience short-term recoveries.

Potential Signals

- Oversold technical conditions
- Improving catalyst score
- Reduced selling pressure
- Stabilizing price action

---

## Low Float Momentum

Hypothesis

Low-float stocks can produce rapid price movements when combined with genuine catalysts.

Potential Signals

- Low float
- High relative volume
- Strong catalyst
- Acceptable liquidity

Because these setups carry elevated risk, stricter risk controls should apply.

---

## Gap Continuation

Hypothesis

Stocks that gap on meaningful news may continue moving in the direction of the gap.

Potential Signals

- Significant opening gap
- Strong catalyst
- Above-average volume
- Positive momentum

---

# Experimental Strategies

The platform should encourage experimentation.

Examples

- AI-generated hypotheses
- Sector-specific strategies
- Seasonal strategies
- Volatility compression
- Earnings continuation
- FDA event reactions

Experimental strategies should remain isolated until validated.

---

# Strategy Evaluation

Every strategy should be measured independently.

Metrics include:

- Win rate
- Average return
- Profit factor
- Maximum drawdown
- Sharpe ratio
- Expectancy
- Average holding period
- Number of trades
- Performance by market condition

No strategy should rely on anecdotal success.

---

# Market Conditions

Strategies should record how they perform in different environments.

Examples:

- Bull markets
- Bear markets
- Sideways markets
- High volatility
- Low volatility
- High interest rates
- Low interest rates

Context matters.

---

# Retirement

Strategies should be retired when they consistently underperform.

Retirement is not failure.

It is evidence that the hypothesis no longer provides value.

Historical performance should remain available for future study.

---

# AI and Strategies

Artificial intelligence should assist strategies by:

- Summarizing supporting evidence
- Identifying conflicting evidence
- Explaining strengths
- Explaining weaknesses
- Comparing historical examples

AI should not invent entry or exit rules without measurable validation.

---

# Position Sizing

Strategies may recommend appropriate risk levels based on:

- Volatility
- Liquidity
- Confidence
- Historical performance

Position sizing guidance should never be presented as a guarantee.

---

# Continuous Improvement

Strategies should evolve through measured evidence.

Possible improvements include:

- Adjusting thresholds
- Refining filters
- Removing weak signals
- Incorporating new data sources
- Reweighting scoring inputs

Every change should be measurable and reversible.

---

# Strategy Independence

Strategies should not depend on each other.

They should share:

- Market data
- Scoring engine
- AI reasoning
- Reporting
- Paper trading

This makes strategies easy to compare and maintain.

---

# Long-Term Vision

Market Intel should become a platform where new strategies can be added, tested, compared, and improved without altering the underlying architecture.

The goal is not to discover one perfect strategy.

The goal is to build a system that continuously identifies which strategies are currently earning their place.

---

# Final Principle

Every strategy begins as an idea.

Only evidence earns it a permanent place in Market Intel.
